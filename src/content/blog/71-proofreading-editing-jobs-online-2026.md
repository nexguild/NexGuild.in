Proofreading is one of the cleaner remote income paths — the work is intellectually engaging, the tools are minimal (just a word processor), and experienced proofreaders earn genuinely good rates. The entry point is lower than most people assume.

## What Proofreaders Actually Do

Proofreading is the final quality check before a document is published. A proofreader reviews:

- **Spelling errors**: including homophone errors (their/there/they're) that spellcheck misses
- **Punctuation**: commas, apostrophes, semicolons, quotation marks
- **Grammar**: subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, dangling modifiers
- **Formatting**: consistent heading styles, line spacing, numbering
- **Consistency**: is the client called "Smith & Co." or "Smith and Co." throughout?

Proofreaders work on blog posts, books, academic papers, business documents, website copy, and legal contracts — wherever written words need to be error-free.

## Skills You Actually Need

**Essential:**
- Excellent command of English grammar and punctuation
- Strong attention to detail (the ability to notice small errors)
- Patience with repetitive checking

**Useful but learnable:**
- Knowledge of style guides (AP Style, Chicago Manual, APA)
- Track Changes in Microsoft Word or Google Docs comment features
- Industry-specific terminology (for specialised niches)

**Not required:**
- English or journalism degree
- Prior employment in publishing

## Where to Find Proofreading Work

### Freelance Marketplaces (Start Here)

**Fiverr**: Create a gig offering proofreading at $5–$25 per 1,000 words. Initial clients come from your gig being discovered; build 5-star reviews quickly by delivering excellent work.

**Upwork**: Apply to posted proofreading jobs. More competitive than Fiverr but often higher-paying clients. Build a profile highlighting any relevant subject expertise.

### Staff Proofreading Platforms

These companies test and hire proofreaders as contractors, then assign client work:

**ProofreadingPal**: Requires passing a proofreading test. Pays per project, typically $600–$1,200/month for consistent work. Takes two proofreaders per document — good for accuracy.

**Scribendi**: Hire freelance editors and proofreaders via application and test. Pays per word for completed projects.

**Gramlee**: Smaller platform, lower rates, but less competitive to get started.

### Direct Client Outreach

Once you have a few portfolio pieces:
- Contact small businesses with poorly proofread websites
- Approach self-publishing authors (Amazon KDP author groups on Facebook)
- Reach out to academic researchers who need proofreading before journal submission
- Partner with marketing agencies that produce high content volume

Direct clients pay more than platforms — typically $25–$50/hour vs. $15–$25/hour through marketplaces.

## Realistic Earning Timeline

**Month 1**: Build Fiverr/Upwork profile. Accept first clients at low rates to build reviews. Earn $50–$200.

**Month 2–3**: Accumulate 10–20 reviews. Raise rates. Earn $200–$500/month.

**Month 4–6**: Develop a niche (legal, medical, academic). Direct client outreach begins. Earn $500–$1,000/month.

**Year 1+**: Established reputation, specialised niche, $1,000–$2,500/month part-time.

## Building a Portfolio Before You Have Clients

The chicken-and-egg problem: clients want samples, but you have no clients.

**Solutions:**
- Proofread publicly available documents (academic papers, blog posts) and annotate the errors you found as a demonstration
- Offer to proofread for local businesses or friends for free in exchange for a testimonial
- Take a structured online course (Proofread Anywhere offers a paid course with assignments)
- Create before/after samples showing a poorly written document corrected
