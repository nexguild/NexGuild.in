Attapoll is a legitimate survey app — but disqualification rates frustrate most new users into quitting before they understand what's actually happening. This guide explains the disqualification logic, what you can realistically earn, and whether it's worth your time in 2026.

## Why Attapoll Keeps Disqualifying You

The most common misconception: disqualifications happen because you answered something "wrong." That's not how it works.

Attapoll acts as a distributor for third-party surveys from market research companies. Each survey has a **target audience specification** — for example, "women aged 25–40 who own a car and live in the UK." When you start a survey, the first 2–4 questions check whether you match that specification. If you don't, you're screened out immediately.

This has nothing to do with your answer quality. It's pure demographic matching. A 22-year-old man cannot complete a survey targeting 35–50-year-old women, no matter how carefully they answer.

**Why your disqualification rate feels high:**
- Attapoll shows you surveys before it knows whether you qualify
- The platform earns a small fee for each completed screener, so showing you surveys even when you're unlikely to qualify is in their interest
- New accounts with incomplete profiles get mismatched more often

## How to Reduce Disqualifications

**Step 1: Complete your profile fully**

Open the Attapoll app → Profile → fill in every section. This includes:
- Age and gender
- Employment status and occupation
- Household income bracket
- Number of people in your household
- Whether you own a car, home, or pets

Attapoll uses this data to pre-filter which surveys you're shown. A complete profile means fewer irrelevant surveys appearing in your queue.

**Step 2: Answer profile questions consistently**

Some surveys ask demographic questions mid-survey to verify your screener answers. If you said you're employed but say "self-employed" mid-survey, it triggers a mismatch flag. Be consistent.

**Step 3: Accept that 40–60% disqualification is normal**

Even on well-optimised accounts, most survey users are disqualified from roughly half the surveys they start. The platforms price their payout rates to compensate for this — completed surveys pay more precisely because many attempts fail.
