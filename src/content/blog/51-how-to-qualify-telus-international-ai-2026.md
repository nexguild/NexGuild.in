## Step 1: Apply Through the Official Portal

Go to the Telus International AI website and find the "Crowd" or "Work For Us" section. Application availability varies by country — not all regions are open at all times. If your region is currently closed, check back monthly; project demand fluctuates.

When applying:
- Use your real name and location — Telus verifies identity
- Select your primary language accurately — project assignments depend on language qualifications
- If asked for experience, be honest — the exam is the real filter, not stated experience

## Step 2: Download the Search Quality Rater Guidelines

Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines are publicly available — search for them directly. The document is 160+ pages. This is not optional background reading. It is, effectively, the entire exam.

The guidelines cover:
- **Needs Met rating** — how well does a search result satisfy the user's likely intent?
- **Page Quality rating** — how authoritative, accurate, and useful is the page itself?
- **Your Money or Your Life (YMYL) pages** — pages on health, finance, legal topics where accuracy requirements are higher
- **Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T)** — the four-factor quality framework
- **Search intent categories** — Know, Do, Website, Visit-in-Person
- **Spam and low-quality content signals**

## Step 3: Study the Guidelines Systematically

The guidelines are dense but structured. A systematic approach works better than reading end-to-end:

**Day 1:** Read Chapters 1–4 (overview, Needs Met rating, understanding queries, understanding pages). These chapters cover the core rating concepts you'll apply most often in the exam.

**Day 2:** Read Chapters 5–13 (Page Quality rating, mobile results, understanding results, rating tasks). Pay particular attention to the Page Quality section — it's the most frequently tested area.

**Day 3:** Review all examples in the guidelines. The examples show correct ratings with explanations. For each example, try to predict the rating before reading the explanation. If you're wrong, study why.

After three days of focused reading, you should be able to:
- Assign a Needs Met rating to any search result with a clear rationale
- Identify High, Medium, and Low Page Quality accurately
- Explain what makes a page YMYL and why it applies a stricter quality standard

## Step 4: Practice Before Taking the Exam

Before attempting the official exam, practice applying ratings to real search results:

1. Pick a search query — any topic
2. Look at the first 5 results
3. For each result, write down: the likely search intent, your Needs Met rating, your Page Quality rating, and your reasoning

Do this for 20–30 queries across different types (informational, navigational, transactional, local). The more you practice writing the reasoning before committing to a rating, the better you'll perform on exam scenarios.

## Step 5: Take the Exam

The Telus International AI qualification exam is typically structured as a series of rating tasks — the same type of evaluation you'll do if hired, but scored against a gold standard.

Key exam tips:
- **Take your time** — the exam is not speed-scored. A careful, well-reasoned rating beats a fast, reflexive one.
- **Refer to the guidelines** during the exam if allowed — re-read the relevant section for each task before committing
- **Don't second-guess yourself into changing correct answers** — your first reasoned judgment based on the guidelines is usually right
- **Focus on the guidelines, not your personal opinion** — the rating that matches the guidelines is the correct one, even if you personally find the result useful

## What Happens After Passing

Passing the qualification exam doesn't mean you start working immediately. After results come through:

- Your profile is added to Telus International AI's qualified contributor pool
- You may receive project assignment immediately, within weeks, or not at all depending on demand in your region
- When assigned, you'll receive access to the project task platform and start receiving hourly work

The waiting period between passing and first assignment is a common frustration. Contributors in high-demand regions (US, UK, Canada, parts of Europe) generally get assigned faster than those in lower-demand markets.
