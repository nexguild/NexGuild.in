Scale AI is one of the most discussed names in the AI training data space — and for good reason. Founded in 2016 by Alexandr Wang and valued at over $13 billion following a 2024 funding round, Scale AI sits at the center of the AI industry's most critical infrastructure need: high-quality human-generated training data. The company counts OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft, and the US government among its clients. For remote contributors looking to earn by contributing to AI development, Scale AI represents one of the higher-paying options in the category — but access is more selective and the experience varies significantly depending on which program you qualify for. This review covers everything you need to understand before applying.

## What Scale AI Does

Scale AI's business is providing AI companies with the labeled data and human feedback they need to build better models. As AI has moved from narrow automation tasks to large language models and multimodal systems, the demand for high-quality human evaluation has grown substantially. Scale AI sits between the AI companies that need this evaluation and the human contributors who provide it.

The types of work Scale AI's contributors perform fall into several categories:

**RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback):** Contributors evaluate AI outputs — rating responses for quality, accuracy, helpfulness, and safety — or create preferred and rejected response pairs that AI models use to improve. This is the work that makes language models like ChatGPT and similar systems better over time. RLHF tasks require genuine judgment and often domain knowledge.

**Data Annotation:** Labeling images, text, video, and other data for machine learning pipelines. This includes bounding boxes on images, sentiment labeling, entity extraction, and content classification. More accessible than RLHF but pays correspondingly less.

**Expert Contribution:** Specialized tasks requiring professional expertise — coding problems and solutions, mathematical proofs, scientific reasoning, legal document analysis. These programs pay the highest rates but are the most selective.

**AI Evaluation and Red-Teaming:** Testing AI systems by identifying weaknesses, biases, or failure modes. Contributors probe AI outputs to find areas where models behave incorrectly or unexpectedly.

## How Scale AI's Contributor Programs Work

Unlike platforms such as Appen or Telus International AI, which have a single unified application process, Scale AI runs multiple distinct contributor programs with different requirements, pay rates, and task types. The program you qualify for depends on your background, skills, and which projects Scale AI has open at the time you apply.

**General contributor programs** — accessible to contributors without specialized credentials, covering standard annotation and evaluation tasks. Entry requirements are lower; pay reflects that.

**RLHF contributor programs** — require demonstrating quality writing, reasoning, and judgment. The application process typically involves sample tasks where your evaluations are assessed for quality. These programs pay meaningfully more than general annotation.

**Domain expert programs** — require demonstrated expertise in specific fields: software engineering, mathematics, science, medicine, law, finance. Scale AI recruits these contributors actively and pays at the highest rates in their contributor tier.

Scale AI's programs open and close based on client project demand. This creates one of the most common contributor experiences: applying during a closed period, waiting for an opening, or qualifying for one program while a more lucrative program remains inaccessible. Active contributor communities track which programs are currently open and what the current application requirements are.

## Pay: What Scale AI Actually Pays

## Getting Accepted: What It Actually Takes

Scale AI's acceptance rate varies by program. General annotation programs are more accessible; RLHF and expert programs are selective. The factors that affect your chances:

**Quality of your sample work.** RLHF application tasks are evaluated for reasoning quality, not just correctness. Vague, surface-level evaluations get rejected even when the answer direction is right. The ability to articulate *why* an AI response is good or bad — specifically, with reference to accuracy, helpfulness, and potential harms — is what qualifies contributors.

**Domain expertise documentation.** Expert programs require evidence of credentials. A software engineering background, academic degree in a STEM field, or professional credentials in law, medicine, or finance are what open these programs. They cannot be substituted with general interest.

**Timing.** Programs open when Scale AI wins new client contracts and close when those contracts' initial phases are staffed. Applying repeatedly over several months until an appropriate program opens is the standard experience for serious applicants.

**Geography.** Scale AI's programs are available globally, but some programs are restricted to specific regions or languages. US-based contributors generally have access to the widest range of programs and the highest-paying task types.

## Scale AI vs Appen vs Telus International AI

All three companies operate in overlapping parts of the AI evaluation space. The practical differences:

**Pay ceiling:** Scale AI is highest, particularly for RLHF and expert programs. Appen and Telus International AI pay $12–$18/hr for search evaluation; Scale AI's top programs pay multiples of that.

**Consistency:** Appen and Telus International AI provide more predictable ongoing hours through search quality rating contracts. Scale AI's project-based structure creates more variability — high availability when a project is active, gaps between projects.

**Accessibility:** Telus International AI's search quality rater exam is demanding but consistently available to apply for. Scale AI's most valuable programs require domain expertise that cannot be learned quickly.

**Task type:** Appen and Telus focus primarily on search evaluation. Scale AI's portfolio includes RLHF, annotation, and expert evaluation — broader in scope.

The standard approach among experienced contributors: apply to Appen and Telus International AI first for reliable baseline hours, then pursue Scale AI's programs as higher-paying supplements.
