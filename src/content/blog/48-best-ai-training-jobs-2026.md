## What AI Training Jobs Actually Involve

The term "AI training" covers a broad range of task types, each requiring different skills and paying at different rates:

**Data labeling and annotation** is the most common entry point. Contributors tag objects in images (bounding boxes, classifications), transcribe audio, label sentiment in text, or categorize content. These tasks require attention to detail and adherence to guidelines, but no technical background.

**AI response rating and evaluation** involves reviewing outputs from AI models — chatbot responses, image generations, code completions — and rating them for quality, accuracy, helpfulness, or safety. Contributors follow detailed rubrics provided by the platform. This pays more than basic labeling because judgment and reading comprehension matter.

**Prompt writing and red-teaming** is the higher end of the spectrum. Contributors write prompts designed to test AI capabilities or expose weaknesses, craft training examples that demonstrate desired behavior, or attempt to get AI systems to produce harmful outputs (red-teaming) to help developers improve safety. This requires domain knowledge and creativity.

**Search relevance and quality evaluation** involves judging whether search results, ads, or AI responses are relevant and high-quality for a given query. UHRS (Microsoft's task platform, accessible through Clickworker) is the largest employer in this category.

**Content moderation** is adjacent to AI training — reviewing flagged content against platform policies. This is psychologically demanding work and typically pays more than basic labeling in recognition of that.

Scale AI is one of the largest AI data infrastructure companies. Focuses on AI response evaluation and conversational AI training. Tasks typically involve rating chatbot responses, writing sample conversations, and identifying errors in AI outputs. Pay ranges from $14–$22/hr for qualified contributors, with higher rates for STEM-domain evaluators.

Clickworker's integration with Microsoft's UHRS platform gives contributors access to search relevance and AI evaluation tasks at enterprise scale. UHRS tasks are among the most consistently available in the AI training category and pay $12–$20/hr for qualified contributors.

## How to Get Started With No Experience

**Step 1 — Join multiple platforms simultaneously.** Appen, Clickworker, and TELUS are all worth applying to at once. Application and approval timelines vary; having multiple accounts active means faster access to work.

**Step 2 — Complete your profile thoroughly.** Language skills, educational background, and areas of expertise all affect which tasks you are eligible for. A complete profile with accurate information matches you to more task types.

**Step 3 — Pass qualification tests carefully.** Most platforms require subject-specific qualification tests before assigning higher-paying tasks. Read the provided guidelines in full before attempting — the tests are based on the guidelines, not general knowledge.

**Step 4 — Start with available tasks, build a track record.** Early work builds your rating and approval rate. Platforms prioritize contributors with strong quality metrics for new task assignments.

**Step 5 — Specialize over time.** Contributors who develop recognizable expertise in a domain — legal, medical, coding, finance — consistently report access to better-paying tasks. This is a medium-term trajectory, not immediate.
