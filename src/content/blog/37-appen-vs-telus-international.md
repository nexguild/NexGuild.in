If you're building income from AI training data work, Appen and Telus International AI are the two names you'll encounter most. Both are legitimate, well-established platforms operating at significant scale. Both focus substantially on search quality evaluation. And both involve the same core skill: reading guidelines carefully, applying consistent judgment, and maintaining accuracy under review. Despite these similarities, the experience of working for each platform is meaningfully different — in qualification requirements, pay consistency, task variety, and how each fits into a sustainable earning strategy. This comparison covers both platforms directly so you can decide where to focus and in what order.

## Company Background

**Appen** is an Australian data company founded in 1996, making it one of the oldest players in the AI training data space. It operates at significant scale — hundreds of thousands of contractors globally — and supplies labeled data to major technology companies including the largest search engines and social media platforms. Appen's business spans search evaluation, social media content review, and broad AI training data annotation.

**Telus International AI** is the AI division of Telus International, a Canadian technology company. It was formerly known as Lionbridge AI before being acquired and rebranded in 2021. Telus International AI focuses primarily on search quality rating — rating whether search results genuinely serve what users were searching for — and has built its reputation on this specific work type.

Both companies have long track records, significant client bases, and are widely regarded as legitimate in the contractor community. Neither is going anywhere.

## Qualification Process

This is one of the most significant practical differences between the two platforms.

**Appen:** Applications are submitted through the Appen Connect portal. Project availability changes regularly, and qualification requirements vary by project. For standard AI annotation work on Appen Crowd, the barrier to entry is low. For the higher-paying search evaluation projects — the ones comparable to Telus International AI's work — Appen requires passing project-specific qualification assessments, but these are generally shorter and less intensive than Telus's exam.

**Telus International AI:** Search quality rating work requires studying Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines — a 160+ page document publicly available for free download — and then passing a multi-part qualification exam. The exam tests your ability to apply the guidelines to real search result scenarios. Most candidates invest two to three days of preparation before attempting it. The exam is a real barrier: you cannot wing it, and candidates who skip the guidelines study regularly fail. After passing, there is typically a waiting period before active project assignment.

**Verdict:** Appen is easier to get started with for immediate, lower-barrier work. Telus International AI's qualification investment pays off with access to consistently higher-quality, better-paid work, but requires upfront study time that Appen's general entry does not.

## Pay Rates

Both platforms pay hourly for their search evaluation work, and the rates for comparable work are broadly similar.

**Appen:** Search evaluation project rates typically fall between $12 and $15 per hour depending on the project and region. Appen Crowd micro-tasks — shorter, simpler annotation jobs — pay significantly less, sometimes in the range of $5–$8 per hour. The platform's broader project mix means pay varies more widely depending on what is currently available in your region.

**Telus International AI:** Search quality rating work typically pays $12 to $18 per hour, with variation by project and region. Payment schedules vary by country but are generally more frequent than Appen's monthly payment cycle in most markets.

**Verdict:** For comparable search evaluation work, pay rates are similar. Telus International AI's ceiling is slightly higher ($18 vs. Appen's typical $15 for search evaluation) and the payment frequency advantage is real in most regions. Appen's micro-task work is more accessible but pays less.

## Task Availability and Consistency

Both platforms share a fundamental limitation: work volume depends on client contracts, and fluctuates in ways no contributor can predict or control.

**Appen:** Availability is project-dependent. When a project is active and you have an open slot, there is work. When a project wraps up or enters a reduced phase, your hours may drop to near zero. Appen has a broader portfolio of project types, which theoretically means more opportunities, but in practice, contributors report significant variability — some periods are very active, others are quiet for weeks.

**Telus International AI:** For approved search quality raters, the work is generally more consistent than Appen during active project phases. The search quality rating work Telus specializes in tends to run on longer project timelines with more predictable weekly hour allocations. That said, no project is permanent — hours can be reduced or suspended when client contracts change.

**Verdict:** Telus International AI offers slightly more predictable hours for approved raters. Appen's broader project range provides more options but with more variability.

## Project Variety

**Appen:** Broader variety is one of Appen's genuine advantages. Beyond search evaluation, Appen contracts include social media content relevance review, speech and audio data collection, image annotation, and a range of AI training data tasks through Appen Crowd. Contractors in some regions have access to multiple simultaneous project types.

**Telus International AI:** More narrowly focused on search quality rating and related AI evaluation work. This focus means the work is more consistent in type — once you understand the search quality guidelines, the learning curve flattens and quality scores tend to improve — but it also means less variety for contributors who prefer diverse tasks.

**Verdict:** Appen offers more variety for contributors who prefer different task types. Telus International AI offers more depth in search evaluation specifically.

## Payment Schedule

**Appen:** Pays monthly via direct bank transfer or payment processors. The monthly cycle is one of the most common complaints from Appen contractors — waiting four weeks for payment requires a different cash flow approach than weekly-paying platforms.

**Telus International AI:** Payment schedules vary by country, but in most markets pay more frequently than Appen's monthly cycle. Many regions receive biweekly or weekly payments, which is a practical advantage for contributors managing cash flow.

**Verdict:** Telus International AI has a clear advantage in payment frequency for most contributors.

## Which Should You Prioritize?

The honest answer is not one or the other — it is both.

The two platforms have enough overlap in the skills they require (reading guidelines, applying consistent judgment, maintaining accuracy) that preparing for one qualifies you for the other. The approach most experienced earners in this category take is:

1. **Start with Appen** to get immediate access to some work while studying for the Telus qualification exam. Appen's lower-barrier entry means you can be earning while you prepare.
2. **Apply to Telus International AI** once you have worked through the Search Quality Rater Guidelines and feel ready for the exam. The exam investment is worth it for the slightly higher rates and more consistent hours.
3. **Run both** once you have active projects on each. This protects against slow periods on either platform and maximizes total available hours.

This sequence gives you earlier income (through Appen's faster entry) while positioning you for the better-paid, more consistent work Telus specializes in.
